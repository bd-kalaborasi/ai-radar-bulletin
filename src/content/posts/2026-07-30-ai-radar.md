### Freehand raises $75M Series B to scale supply-chain AI agents at Fortune 500

**Source:** https://www.freehand.ai/press-release/freehand-debuts-ai-supply-chain-spend-management · Freehand · 2026-07-29
**Verification:** T2 verified · funding announcement · workflow-automation

Freehand raised $75 million in Series B funding on 29 July 2026, co-led by Battery Ventures and NewRoad Capital Partners with participation from Nexus Venture Partners and former US Commerce Secretary Penny Pritzker, bringing total capital to $100 million. The San Francisco startup deploys AI agents that autonomously manage supply-chain spend inside enterprise systems — reading supplier contracts, negotiating rates, approving invoices, and reconciling records without per-step human approval. Named current clients include Meta, Unilever, Johnson & Johnson, Pfizer, Dunkin', and Cardinal Health. Reported outcomes across clients: 5–10% spend recovery, workflows 5–7× faster, and procure-to-pay cycles reduced by more than 70%.

**Why it matters for automation/productivity:** Freehand is one of the most concrete live examples of autonomous AI agents executing end-to-end enterprise workflows. Named Fortune 500 clients and specific outcome figures make it an evaluable deployment reference for organizations assessing supply-chain or back-office agent use cases.

**Key claims:**

### Hush Security raises $30M Series A to govern enterprise AI agent identities

**Source:** https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hush-security-raises-30m-to-close-the-ai-agent-governance-gap-with-akamai-joining-as-strategic-investor-302836307.html · PR Newswire · 2026-07-28
**Verification:** T2 verified · funding announcement · ai-for-business / agent-framework

Tel Aviv-based Hush Security closed a $30 million Series A on 28 July 2026, backed by Battery Ventures, YL Ventures, and Akamai Technologies as strategic investor, bringing total capital to $41 million. Founded in 2024 and emerged from stealth in September 2025, Hush provides a machine-access platform that enrolls AI agents in a central registry, issues scoped just-in-time permissions at runtime, eliminates stored credentials, logs every agent action, and offers a centralized kill switch for rapid shutdown. The company targets the non-human identity problem created by proliferating multi-agent deployments: Hush's press release cites a Gartner projection that the average Fortune 500 will run 150,000+ AI agents by 2028, up from fewer than 15 today.

**Why it matters for automation/productivity:** Enterprises deploying AI agents across sensitive enterprise systems face an identity and access management gap that existing IAM tools were not designed for. JIT-permission, credential-free, auditable governance with a kill switch is a prerequisite for deploying agents in regulated environments at scale — relevant when scoping any multi-agent production architecture.

**Key claims:**
- $30M Series A; $41M total raised → PR Newswire primary
- JIT permissions at runtime, credential elimination, centralized kill switch → PR Newswire primary
- Gartner: Fortune 500 will run 150,000+ agents by 2028, up from <15 today → PR Newswire (Gartner cited in release; original Gartner report not independently retrieved)

**Cross-references:**
- https://www.securityweek.com/hush-security-raises-30-million-for-ai-agent-governance/ (T2-T3, corroborating)

**Caveats:** Gartner forecast cited via press release only; original Gartner research not independently verified in this run.

### Claude Mythos Preview discovers two cryptographic attack improvements at ~$100k per finding

**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses · Anthropic · 2026-07-28
**Verification:** T2 verified · research publication · research-papers
**COI:** Anthropic reports on its own unreleased model; no independent reproduction of findings confirmed in this run.

Anthropic published research on 28 July 2026 showing Claude Mythos Preview — an unreleased model — improved known attacks on two cryptographic targets through semi-autonomous AI agent collaboration at approximately $100,000 per finding in API compute costs. The first target was HAWK, a post-quantum digital signature scheme under NIST review for two years without deployment: Mythos identified a previously unknown automorphism in HAWK's lattice structure that expert review had missed, reducing the key recovery attack cost from 2^64 to 2^38 operations in 60 hours of autonomous work. The second finding introduced a Möbius Bridge fingerprinting algorithm that makes a meet-in-the-middle attack on 7-round reduced AES-128 between 200 and 800 times faster than the prior best, at comparable cost. Neither result threatens production systems — HAWK is undeployed and the AES finding targets a 7-round research variant, not the 10-round standard used in practice.

**Why it matters for automation/productivity:** At a $100k compute budget, a semi-autonomous AI agent found a novel weakness in a well-scrutinized cryptographic scheme that human experts reviewed for two years without discovering. Organizations considering AI agents for adversarial code review or security auditing have a concrete cost-and-capability benchmark. Anthropic's own observation — that AI cryptanalysis capability may soon outpace human capacity to verify findings — is a direct deployment constraint for security-critical agent workflows.

**Key claims:**
- HAWK attack cost reduced: 2^64 → 2^38 key recovery operations → Anthropic primary
- AES 7-round attack: 200–800× faster than prior best → Anthropic primary
- API compute cost: ~$100,000 per attack finding → Anthropic primary
- HAWK discovery time: 60 hours of semi-autonomous work → Anthropic primary
- Methodology: multi-worker AI agents using Python, Sage, literature review, experimental validation → Anthropic primary

**Cross-references:**
- https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/28/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses-with-claude/ (T2, corroborating — hands-on commentary)
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/claude-ai-just-cracked-post-quantum.html (T3, corroborating)

**Caveats:** Vendor-conducted research using an unreleased model; no independent team has reproduced findings. Both attacks target non-production systems (HAWK undeployed; AES attack on 7-round research variant only).

### *Pacing the Frontier*: 1,273 frontier AI workers request US-backed development pacing tools

**Source:** https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/ · Guidelight AI Standards / Encode AI · 2026-07-28
**Verification:** T2 verified · open letter · policy-regulation

An open letter titled *Pacing the Frontier*, published 28 July 2026, collected signatures from 1,273 employees of frontier AI companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta AI. High-profile signatories include Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI Chief Research Officer Mark Chen, Meta AI Chief Scientist Shengjia Zhao, and Google VP of AI Safety and Alignment Anca Dragan. OpenAI and Anthropic each endorsed the letter as corporate organizations on 28–29 July. The letter asks the US government to back an international effort to build the technical and governance tools capable of deliberately slowing frontier AI development — explicitly not calling for an immediate pause, but requesting infrastructure that would make a coordinated pacing mechanism possible if AI capabilities advance faster than human oversight capacity.

**Why it matters for automation/productivity:** Informational only for immediate workflow decisions. For organizations planning multi-year AI adoption roadmaps, the letter is a leading indicator: if a US-led pacing framework materializes, it could introduce delays in frontier capability releases and affect when specific model capabilities become available for deployment. A critical perspective published in Forbes on 29 July frames the letter as incumbents shaping rules to their competitive advantage — a relevant lens for interpreting the stated intent.
